**Outcome.** A run that fails several checks at once tells Maya which personas and journeys it breaks, worst first. She fixes the path her readers hit first, not the file that sorts first.

**Steps.** Her trigger is a refactor that turns a few dozen citations red, plus a stray to-do marker on a page she was rewording. She runs `manni check`. Each domain prints its own report, as before. After the skipped lines, one block ranks the personas and journeys the red pages serve, by errors, then warnings, then notices. A draft or archived journey sorts after the live ones. She takes the top journey and asks for its pages with `manni check -f json | jq '.impact[] | select(.id == "m1") | .pages[]'`. She fixes those pages and runs the check again. The journey leaves the list, and every journey that shared those pages leaves with it. On a pull request, `-f github` opens with the same ranking as one notice, so the cap on annotations never hides it.

**What success looks like.** The first fix clears the journey her readers hit first, and the rest of the run is ranked for whoever owns it. Related journeys are D16 (the one-step gate), M5 (pins to source) and T1 (fixing a failing check).
