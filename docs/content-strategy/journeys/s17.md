**Outcome.** The audiences, personas and journeys Sara's docs serve are records, and every page that names one is checked against them. A journey no page serves shows up as a finding, and "which pages serve S5?" is one command.

**Steps.** Sara's trigger is a page that names `journeys: [m55]` and passes every check, because nothing says whether M55 exists. She keeps the strategy as one file per record under `docs/content-strategy/`. An audience is a page with `type: audience`. A persona has `type: persona` and `audience:`. A journey has `type: journey` and `personas:`, the one or more personas it serves. She adds a `strategy` collection for those files to `manni.config.yaml`. `manni strategy list` prints the tree, with the number of pages that name each record. `manni strategy get m5` matches the id in any case. It prints the record, its parents and children, and the pages that name it. `manni strategy check` reads `audiences:`, `personas:` and `journeys:` on every page against the records, whether the page or its sidecar holds them. A value no record defines is an `undefined-reference` and fails the run. The message adds a did-you-mean. When the value names a record of another type, it names that record's own parent instead. A field with no records of its type is not checked, so a repository can keep personas and leave audiences free-form. A page that names a persona without its audience, or a journey without any of its personas, draws a `missing-parent` warning. A journey that no page names draws an `unserved-journey` warning, and she clears it on the pages that serve the journey. Setting `strategy.severity.unserved-journey: error` raises it, so no journey lands without a page. `manni check` runs the same check beside the other domains, and `manni status` shows whether the records are in play.

**What success looks like.** The records and the pages agree, and the check says so on every push. A typo in `personas:` fails with the name Sara meant. Related journeys are S1 (the standard), S6 (the term pages), S15 (the vocabulary) and D16 (the one-command gate).
