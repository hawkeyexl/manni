Structure linting has a blank-page problem.

The template format is strict by default, which is the right default: a section
the template does not describe is a finding, not a shrug. But it means pointing
the linter at a docset you already have gives you a wall of "unexpected
section" before it gives you anything useful. So you go and write the template
by hand, from nothing, for every doctype you own.

`manni lint templates infer` starts you somewhere else. Point it at one page
that already has the shape you want, and it writes the template that describes
that page: the headings, in order, and the content each section actually
carries. Save it, and the same lint command that produced the wall now passes.

It is a first draft, and it says so. One page cannot show what varies, so every
rule it writes is exactly one occurrence. Loosening a heading, marking a
section optional with `min: 0`, and adding a trailing rule for whatever else
may follow are your edits to make. That is the point: you are editing a
template instead of starting one.

43 seconds, no audio needed. Everything in the frame is a real run against the
repository's own test fixtures.
